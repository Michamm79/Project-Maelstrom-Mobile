## What it is, accurately

A touch-first mobile build of **Project Maelstrom**, an original game design.
You wake in a bounded arena with no memory and no explanation. Gauntlets on both
hands pull material out of the world as you walk; you craft that into permanent
upgrades, break it into elements, and recombine those into combat abilities. The
world notices you eventually.

It is a **complete, playable vertical slice** — roughly 30–60 minutes of
content — not a commercially released title. Say that plainly; it is a stronger
portfolio piece as an engineering showcase than as a product claim.

## The engineering worth writing about

A portfolio page should pick **two or three** of these. Each has a real measured
number attached, which is what makes them interesting rather than generic.

### 1. Everything is generated — there are no assets

The character sprite is produced from character maps and a colour palette. The
38 material icons are procedural shapes with a dilated outline, banded shading
and a baked drop shadow. The three enemy tiers are hand-authored vector
drawings, posed by wind-up and stagger values rather than animation frames. All
sound is oscillators and shaped noise through envelopes.

**Why it matters:** the entire game is 43KB over the wire — smaller than a
single mid-sized JPEG. That is the reason it installs instantly and works
offline, and it is a deliberate constraint rather than a limitation.

### 2. The enemy AI was a lock-on wearing a disguise

The design called for the player to have the informational advantage — the
system does not know where the intruder is. The code had a field called
`aggroRadius` set to 220–360 world units, which covered most of a 315×700
screen. Inside it, an enemy walked the exact line to the player's current
position until it was outrun.

Rewritten: enemies wander between roam targets, notice only at an **encounter
distance of 90–140 units**, and forget once you break away. The content build
now *fails* on a notice radius above 200, on the grounds that anything wider is
a detection sweep rather than an encounter.

### 3. The regions were a colour palette and a loot table

Each of the five biomes stated a mechanical identity in its own description —
"cover in every direction", "nowhere to hide", "slow going" — and **none of it
was implemented.** The Wetland played identically to the Desert.

Now walking speed, concealment, sight range and fog are per-region values that
blend across the border. The Wetland walks at 0.78 speed with 0.6 concealment;
the Desert has 1.45 concealment, so enemies spot you from half again as far.
Scenery was also 1.7 props per screen across 116 million square units, which is
why every region read as bare ground — raised to about 17.

### 4. Forcing landscape on a phone that will not turn

There is no web API that turns a phone sideways. `screen.orientation.lock()` is
the right answer and needs a user gesture plus either fullscreen or an installed
app; Safari does not implement it at all; and **none of it helps the player who
has rotation lock switched on**, which is the one case where "just turn your
phone" is a dead end rather than advice.

So the game asks in three steps and falls back to rotating its own box with a
CSS transform. The fallback is where the work is, because a rotated element's
bounding rect is its *axis-aligned cover*: everything that measured one got the
axes silently swapped. Three separate bugs, each of which looked fine in a
screenshot:

- The canvas backing store came out **portrait inside a landscape box**, which
  is the same stretched art the rotation was supposed to fix.
- The joystick steered sideways — dragging left walked the player up.
- `@media (orientation: landscape)` kept answering about the phone rather than
  the box, so the compact HUD layout never engaged; and `max-height: 94vh` on
  the menu resolved against the *phone*, making it 860px tall inside a 412px
  box and pushing its own tabs off the screen.

Fixed by having the box publish its own shape and size, and mapping every touch
through one inverse transform. A headless-browser check drives the rotated
layout with real multi-touch and asserts that a thumb dragged toward the phone's
bottom edge walks the player *left*.

### 5. Measuring instead of guessing

A recurring theme, and the most honest thing to highlight:

- The attack "felt weightless". The knockback value of 40 moved an enemy about
  **6 world units against a 42-unit body** — invisible. Fixed with a stagger so
  the shove lands whole: measured 39 units.
- Frame rate dropped 10fps in fogged regions. Profiling showed the scenery and
  the world objects were free; **the fog was the entire cost**, being one
  full-screen alpha blend. It is now baked small and stretched, and it fades out
  under sustained load and returns when there is headroom.
- A rotation bug looked like a layout problem. It was the canvas backing store
  never resizing — measured, rotating a portrait page stretched the art
  **2.22× horizontally and 0.45× vertically**.
- "The view feels cramped" turned out to be one number. The camera showed 700
  world units across the long axis, which on a phone held sideways is
  **700×315** with a 63px character standing in it. Raised to 900 — two thirds
  more ground each way, character still 49px — and measured at three view
  settings in the fogged region: **16.7ms a frame at all three**, because the
  scenery is bucketed and the fog does not scale with zoom.

### 6. Content is validated, not trusted

Game content lives in JSON and passes through a build step that refuses to emit
anything that breaks the design's own rules: an element symbol that collides
with the real periodic table, a material scattered outside its own region, a
tutorial step that would require travel, an enemy with a loot drop, a region
with no terrain. CI fails if the committed bundle has drifted from source.

## Do not claim

Accuracy matters more than impressiveness here. Specifically:

- ❌ **Not published on an app store.** It is a web build on GitHub Pages.
- ❌ **No player base, downloads or revenue.** Do not invent metrics.
- ❌ **Not a finished game.** It is a vertical slice of roughly 30–60 minutes.
- ❌ **Not a team project** unless the site says otherwise — describe the actual
  working arrangement.
- ❌ **Do not call the art "AI-generated images".** It is procedural code —
  drawing instructions, not image generation. The distinction is real and a
  technical reader will notice.
- ⚠️ **The `unity/` directory in the repo targets the wrong engine** and is
  documented as such. Do not present it as a shipped Unity port.
